# The rectangular decision mesh (23 September 2026)

The rectangular mesh fits the same thing as the triangular one, a smooth logit correction over
loan age and coupon to pool termination counts, with the same statistical machinery, on a
different geometry: rectangles cut in half along either axis, with bilinear interpolation inside
each cell. This writeup explains what that geometry changes, how it was built into a working
estimator, and how it compares. The engineering detail (every decision with its measurement) is
`docs/RECT_ESTIMATOR.md`; the triangular estimator is described in
`triangular-decision-mesh/docs/WRITEUP_2026-09-23.md`, and everything statistical below is that
estimator unless said otherwise.

## 1. Why rectangles

A triangular mesh refined by bisection has one fixed direction of refinement per triangle, and
its piecewise-linear surface is tied to the diagonal the first split happened to choose. A
rectangle can be cut along either axis, so the refinement can follow the data's anisotropy: the
termination rate changes quickly with age over the first months and slowly after, and changes
with coupon mainly through the refinancing incentive. The gate decides which axis to cut, one
candidate per direction, instead of the geometry deciding for it. Bilinear cells also carry the
interaction of the two coordinates inside a cell, where a triangle is linear.

The price is conformity. Refining one rectangle leaves its neighbour with a vertex in the middle
of an edge (a hanging vertex), and no finite completion removes it the way bisection completes a
triangular split. The August design notes (`docs/GEOMETRY_DESIGN.md`) worked out how to live
with that; the implementation below simplified it.

## 2. The geometry as built

Every vertex except the four corners of the square is born bisecting a dyadic segment and has
exactly two parents, the segment's endpoints. Its height is the average of its parents' heights
plus its surplus, and only a free coefficient has a surplus. That one rule does three jobs:

- **Continuity without constraints.** A hanging vertex lies on the edge of a coarser cell, and its
  parents lie on that same edge, so its height is exactly that edge's linear trace and the
  surface is continuous across it. No constraint table is stored and no balance rule is needed.
- **Invisible geometry.** A cut whose new vertices are not free reproduces the old surface
  exactly (bilinear refinement is nested). The mesh can therefore be refined wherever a
  candidate needs it without changing the fit.
- **Canonical parents.** A point on a vertical cell boundary at height (odd) x 2^-k can only be
  born bisecting the segment of length 2^(1-k) centred on it, whichever neighbour is cut first.
  A candidate's future parents are known before any cut is made.

A free coefficient's design column is its hierarchical hat: its influence passed to every
descendant at one half per generation and read bilinearly in the leaf cells. The column never
changes after the coefficient is born, so the triangular engine's dormancy, retirement and
two-node machinery have nothing to do here.

## 3. The candidates

A round offers every constrained vertex and the midpoint of every leaf edge that has no vertex
yet. A midpoint on a vertical edge is a cut of the cells on either side along the other axis, so
every cell offers both of its cuts. Admitting a candidate splits the cells around it until it is
a corner of each (the birth of a new vertex, or the release of a hanging one), which leaves the
surface unchanged, and then frees its coefficient. The candidate's column is computed before any
of that: through its existing descendants where it is already a corner, and as the plain bilinear
hat on the cell the move will create where it is not. A test on random meshes checks that this
prediction matches the realised column to 1e-12.

Everything after the column is the triangular gate: the centred, refit-adjusted and
shrinkage-corrected score, the Lindsey empirical null per round, admission of the prefix whose
running mean lfdr stays below 0.10, re-profiling at admission, and the EM depth variance.

## 4. Two changes the rectangles asked for

**Nodal coordinates.** A point lies under a few hierarchical hats per level, so the design rows
in the surplus basis ran to 28 entries, against at most 7 in the nodal basis, where a free
vertex's column stops at its free descendants. The engine fits the free vertices' heights with the
nodal design and expresses the depth prior on the surpluses as a dense penalty. It is the same
model: all 72 battery fits made identical admissions and gave identical held-out deviances. But
the products that scale with the row width squared shrank from about 1.2 s to 0.1 s of a
single-threaded fit, which is what made the rectangular engine faster than the triangular one.

**Two guards in the solver.** Rows whose linear predictor moved less than 0.01 logit reuse cached
moments, linearised, and switch to exact moments past that. On one fit in the first battery the
line search stalled on such a switch. The solver now re-bases the cache when Newton stalls and
continues, falling back to exact moments; a converging fit never reaches it. And the check that
the prior's penalty is positive semidefinite is now relative to its scale: the nodal prior mixes
precisions up to 4e4 with small ones, and an eigenvalue at or near zero can round a little
negative.

## 5. Settings, chosen by measurement

The prior's depth is the level of the bisected segment: a surplus corrects the interpolation
along its segment, so its expected size scales with that segment's length. The fit starts from an
8 x 8 grid with the corners and the midlines free, the rest of the grid waiting on the frontier.

Every alternative the rectangular geometry opens was run on four tests: the year battery (random
splits, the criterion), the **cohort split** (the newest issue months held out, so the fit must
extrapolate to young loans; every month of 2020), the 120,595-row Ginnie design and the SMM design.
The random battery could not tell most alternatives apart; the other tests decided three things.

- **A prior on the four corner heights** (sd 1 logit around the baseline). With flat corners, as
  in the triangular engine, the rectangular fit extrapolated to new cohorts 6.6% worse than the
  triangular one: an empty corner of a bilinear surface goes wherever the coarse structure pushes
  it. With the prior the cohort split is level (-1.8%, se 1.9%), at no cost on the battery.
- **A larger starting backbone was rejected** after looking like a win. Freeing the quarter-grid
  vertices too improved the Ginnie fits by 0.57% and fixed two SMM seeds, but those free heights
  at the edge of the data extrapolated steeply into held-out young cohorts: +139% on the cohort
  split. It was the default for about an hour.
- **The empirical null's scale is capped at 3**, not the triangular gate's 6. In the first round
  of one SMM fit nearly every candidate carried signal, the null's fit took the signal for a null
  of sd 4, and the gate admitted nothing and stopped. The cap binds on no other fit here, and on
  the triangular engine it changes no single-issuer fit and improves three pooled ones.

A separate prior variance per axis, a generation count instead of the segment level, coarser or
finer starting grids, a minimum number of effective pools per candidate, a fine node, and four
other ways of estimating the depth variance came out within noise or worse. The table is in
`docs/RECT_ESTIMATOR.md`.

On the criterion, the single-issuer year battery, the rectangular engine is on par, slightly
ahead. It is level on the cohort split and in the combined predictor (the surface transferred to
the multi-issuer pools with levels and cohort fields, the main population model), a little behind
on the Ginnie design, and level on SMM but for one seed, where a coefficient admitted in thin data
makes a spike that one held-out pool sits on. Pooled means are decided by giant pools in a few
splits and move a long way with small changes to either engine.

## 7. Two implementations

`core/` is the C++ reference; `pyrect/` is a numpy/scipy port that runs the same command line,
reads the same environment and writes the same files. Both are started from the triangular
repo's code for everything that does not touch the geometry. They agree to 1e-9 on every dump:
on the pinned fits most tables are identical to the last printed digit, and all 72 battery fits
give identical held-out deviances. The binary's output does not depend on its thread count.
`scripts/pyrect_engine` makes the port usable wherever the binary is, including the triangular
repo's benchmark harness.

## 8. Star plots

`python3 -m analysis.starplot <run dir> <design.csv> --vertex <id>` draws one admission the way
the triangular tool does: on the plane, the cells the coefficient's hierarchical column reaches,
its pools sized by their share of the coefficient's information and coloured by their residual;
two sections through the vertex, along its parent segment (the parents at -1 and +1) and across
it (+-1 at the edge of the column), clipped to the square, with observed log-odds by bin against
the fit, the null with the surplus removed, and the model's 68% band; and the card (the admission,
the correction against the prior and the data alone, the evidence, the held-out pools, and a
compared run of the same design). The page below is the largest correction on the February fit,
compared with the triangular engine's fit of the same split: newly issued loans at 2.5 months
prepay far below the baseline, and the rectangular fit puts a -6.8 logit correction there, which
the data alone would put at -8.3.

![a star page](figures/star_vertex149.png)

The card also says what only a rectangular mesh has. The engine logs every candidate each gate
round scored (`run_candidates.csv`), and each admission's move and the far ends of its cut,
recorded when it is admitted (`admit_move`, `admit_splits`, `admit_chord_lo`, `admit_chord_hi` in
the vertex table). The card uses these for three sections:

- The admission section adds the vertex's rank in its round and its move. The move is one of
  three: a new midpoint with the cells split to make it a corner; an existing vertex promoted
  with nothing split; or a hanging vertex released.
- THE CUT gives the segment it bisects in months or coupon points, and the direction the cut
  runs. It also gives the other way to split the same cells: the candidates on those cells' other
  midline in the same round, the best one's score, and what became of it. A near tie is flagged.
  The last line gives the cut's far ends, as recorded at admission, and whether each is now free,
  constrained or hanging.
- COLUMN gives the column's extent and its aspect, and how many of its cells are the vertex's own
  against those reached through descendants. It also gives the share of the column's variation
  that the free descendants carry, and how many descendants hang on a coarser edge.

The prior line gives the depth's segment length, and for a corner the root prior (sd 1 logit).
On the SMM fit, 39 of the 44 admissions had a competing cut of the same cells in their round, and
6 were near ties. The page below is one of the others: a released hanging vertex whose competing
cut scored 3.3 against its 5.9.

![a star page on the SMM fit](figures/star_smm_vertex94.png)

## 9. What is open

- A fine node, the rectangular counterpart of the two-node hierarchy, was built and measured
  (-0.07%, se 0.04%, on the single-issuer battery) and not kept: the two-node hierarchy repaired a
  loss of coarse roles that the rectangular hierarchical basis does not suffer. The formulation
  that works is recorded in `docs/RECT_ESTIMATOR.md`.
- The statistical items the two meshes share. The first is the depth variance estimated from gate
  winners. Four corrections were tried here and none helped; the most telling one estimated it
  from every scored candidate, with no selection at all, and made the battery 0.78% worse. The
  gate and EM together act like a spike-and-slab prior; an explicit null/slab mixture fitted to
  each round's candidates was worse still (+0.37%), because a round's candidate estimates are
  strongly dependent. EM on the winners was never beaten (`docs/RECT_ESTIMATOR.md`). The others
  are a pool variance per population and the giant pools.
- Coefficients admitted in thin data can make local spikes (the SMM seed that trails, the dips
  near 60 months in the figure); the deep levels' prior variance is large, so they are barely
  shrunk.
- Three changes made here apply to the triangular engine as they are, for its owner to take or
  leave: the null cap at 3 (on the triangular engine it changes no single-issuer fit and improves
  three pooled ones), counting a round with no actual admission as a stall (the triangular SMM
  fit, seed 1, idles for 70 rounds without it), and the stalled-Newton fallback.
- The triangular star inspector (the interactive page) has no rectangular version yet. The star
  plot does (`analysis/starplot.py`, below), and `scripts/plot_fit.py` draws a whole fit.
